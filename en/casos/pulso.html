PULSO

A training app the gym offers its members to log sets and weights during their workout, without relying on memory or phone notes.

Role

UX/UI designer · personal project

Type

Design proposal · not yet validated with users

Scope

Mobile app · 46 screens and clickable prototype

Duration

1 week · Figma

(01) Context and problem

The starting point

When I wanted to track my progress at the gym, I couldn't remember what weight or how many reps I'd done the last time. I ended up writing it in my phone's notes, which isn't practical in the middle of a workout.

Design question

How could someone log their progress during a workout, set by set, without relying on memory?

Business hypothesis

It happens to me and to people close to me. One of them pays for a separate subscription to log workouts, when the gym itself could include that tool in its membership. Hence the model: the gym offers the app and its members use it.

Based on my own and close experience · not yet validated

(02) User and scope

Designed to be used between sets

01

The moment of use

Whoever is training logs each set and its weight right after finishing it, with one or two hands, without leaving the workout.

02

What was left out

Social network, chat, bookings and AI. They aren't essential to logging progress; a community could be added in a future version.

03

Ask only for what's needed

A short, skippable onboarding, with no body data or location: they aren't needed for the core use.

(03) Design decisions

Eight decisions for friction-free training

01

“Train” at the center

The app is opened mostly once the workout has started and the user wants to log progress; that's why this action is the most visible in the navigation.

02

No tab bar during the session

Mid-workout, the priority is the exercise, switching machines and logging. More options would be a distraction.

03

Target in gray, actual result with ✓

The target is a medium- or long-term goal; the result is today's progress. Confirming a set unlocks the next one.

04

Copy the previous set

Repeating the last set to keep the pace is common, so it can be copied with one tap without marking it as done.

05

Start from a saved routine

Before training, users pick an existing routine instead of building one from scratch, so they can compare progress session by session.

06

Alternatives without weight equivalence

If a machine is taken, the app suggests other exercises but doesn't calculate how much weight to use: there's no general equivalence, and giving one would be irresponsible.

07

Consistency without guilt

Weekly frequency informs without pressuring. A rest day or something unexpected shouldn't make anyone feel bad.

08

No trends with little data

With few sessions, trends swing too much to be helpful. The app shows the data and users decide based on their own goals.

(04) Home

The most refined screen

It shows the next routine with a shortcut, recent routines, the last session and the current week. It has its own empty state for people who haven't created a routine yet.

A

Just two steps

Name and exercises: that's all a routine needs. Fields like “how did you feel” don't affect the core and could confuse users.

B

Filter by body area

Many people don't know every exercise's name, but they do know whether they want chest, back or legs. The library combines search and filters.

(07) Design system

01

Components for what repeats

Whatever appears again and again —set rows, routine cards, buttons, navigation— is a component. I gained consistency and speed when designing new screens.

02

Hierarchy that guides

The 8 px grid, generous touch targets and large buttons organize the screen and lead the eye to what matters.

03

Errors based on what's at stake

The pattern —toast, banner, full screen or confirmation— depends on how important the affected feature is.

(08) Validation and limits

01

No user testing yet

The prototype hasn't been tested. The next step is a usability test with Maze on the main flows.

02

Placeholder images

The exercise library uses temporary thumbnails that should be replaced with a consistent visual set.

03

Sample data

Names, dates and charts are fictional and don't represent real results.

(09) Learnings

What I'd do differently: define the app's identity before designing the first screen.

The visual direction changed several times because I didn't have a clear idea of the brand from the start. Along the way I learned to work with styles and variables, so I could adjust the palette without redoing every screen. It's the part of the project I most want to strengthen.
