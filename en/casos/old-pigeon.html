Old Pigeon

An augmented reality app that turns any walk into an experience: point the camera at a building and discover its history, architecture and curiosities.

Role

UX/UI designer

Type

Academic project reviewed by faculty

Duration

5 weeks

Methods

Benchmark, personas, card sorting, interaction score

(01) Problem

The starting point

When exploring a new city, it's hard to quickly find reliable historical and architectural information. Travelers and design professionals want more than pictures: they want context, history and accurate facts without relying on tour guides or long online searches.

(02) Benchmark

What's on the market

We analyzed similar apps to understand Old Pigeon's positioning and improve its value proposition, assessing accessibility, user experience and information quality.

(03) User definition

A curious traveler

The profiles were based on interviews and usability tests. The user is a modern traveler, passionate about getting to know the world in depth: they want more than a photo — stories, facts and context that enrich their trip.

(04) Card sorting and sitemap

How users group information

Card sorting showed how users group and prioritize information within the app; that order became the sitemap.

(05) Wireframes and testing

Test before designing

Low-fidelity wireframes defined the structure and flow. The interaction score technique showed how users moved through the app.

01

Find which devices the app works on

Participant 1 · 26, tourist. Estimated time: 2 min. Completed the task without issues in 1:13 min.

02

Locate Parque Universitario

Participant 2 · 24, photographer. Estimated time: 2 min. Struggled because search wasn't enabled, but completed the task in 1:42 min.

Test conclusion

Navigation proved fairly intuitive, but the menu structure and access to secondary features needed improvement.

(06) High fidelity

What changed after testing

01

Reorganized menu

Information was reordered to make finding points of interest easier.

02

Visible search

Search was enabled and highlighted on the main screen — the point that held one participant back.

03

Visual hierarchy

A clearer layout of elements for more intuitive reading.

(07) Aprendizajes

A well-structured, easy-to-navigate interface can be worth more than a striking look.

The process reaffirmed the importance of designing around the user and validating decisions with real tests before moving to the final stages.
